2. Scholarship Exam Module (HOSE) — Delivered August 2026

Built for the offline scholarship exams — HAMSE and HACKSE — sat on the institute’s 60-question OMR sheet. A school sends its student list as a spreadsheet; the system registers every student, issues roll numbers, reads the scanned answer sheets, scores them, works out the scholarship each student has earned, and hands them back to the counsellors as live leads. Each exam is separate, with its own paper, answer keys and roll-number block.

StageStatusWhat was delivered
School list uploadDELIVERED The institute’s own 49-column lead template is read directly — no re-typing. Every upload is previewed before anything is saved, showing exactly how many students are new, how many already exist, and which rows were rejected and why. Rejected rows download as a spreadsheet the school can correct and re-send. A student listed twice, or already on file from another school, stays one person.
Roll numbersDELIVERED Six digits, generated automatically, never reused. The last digit is a check digit: the roll number is the only machine-readable mark on the sheet, so a single misread bubble would otherwise hand one child’s marks to another. The check digit turns that into a visible “review this sheet” instead. Roll numbers issued to a school already exist on their hall tickets, so a block can be widened but never moved out from under them.
Roll list & hall ticketsDELIVERED Printable invigilator roll list with a signature column and present/absent footer, and one hall ticket per candidate carrying the roll number, centre, date, photo box and the exam instructions — including that marking is +4 / −1, so guessing costs marks.
Exam setupDELIVERED Per exam: the answer-sheet layout, number of questions, marking scheme, roll-number block, the subject split you choose yourself (e.g. 1–10 one subject, 11–30 the next), and four answer keys, one per booklet A–D. The screen refuses a split that leaves a question in no subject or in two, and refuses a partial answer key — both would quietly mis-score every candidate. A checklist shows what still stands between the exam and scanning.
OMR sheet readingCALIBRATION PENDING Reads the 60-question sheet: all answers, the 6-digit roll number, the booklet code, board and exam name. Both edges of the page are used to straighten a crooked scan rather than merely tolerate it. Built and tested end-to-end; thresholds must still be tuned against about 20 real filled sheets before the first live exam — see the note below.
Scanning & scoringDELIVERED Upload a batch of scanned sheets and each is scored against the answer key for the booklet that student actually bubbled. Nothing is ever guessed: a sheet with no readable roll, a failed check digit, a roll not in this exam, a duplicate, or a blank booklet bubble goes to a review queue with its scanned image attached, to be identified by hand. Candidates with no sheet at all are marked absent.
Scholarship calculationDELIVERED The full brochure scheme. HAMSE awards a scholarship equal to the score percentage, HACKSE 90% of it, capped at 100% and 90%. Also covers school exam score, extraordinary performance, previous NEET score, and the non-academic categories (ex-student, single parent, sibling, defence, police, martyr) — 24 rules in all. Per the brochure, a student receives the single highest route they qualify for, never the sum, and non-exam claims count only once their proof has been verified. The winning rule is recorded, so every figure can be explained.
Results & exportsDELIVERED Ranked result list with subject-wise marks, percentage and scholarship; a printable PDF result slip per candidate; and an Excel export in the institute’s own 49-column template, so results drop straight back into the sheet the schools already use.
Hand-off to counsellorsDELIVERED Publishing results moves every candidate into the existing sales pipeline at a new Result Declared stage, carrying their scholarship percentage. The counsellor’s normal follow-up and admission flow picks them up unchanged, and the concession is ready for the fee plan. Students already admitted are deliberately left untouched, so a fee ledger already agreed is never rewritten.
Result message to parentsREADY — MANUAL Composes the result and scholarship message per candidate and sends it over WhatsApp on an approved template. Held at manual send at the institute’s request — the button currently previews the exact message instead of sending. Switching to automatic sending is a single setting.

Before the first live exam: the sheet reader has been built and proven against generated sheets, but not yet against real scanned paper. Please provide about 20 filled sheets covering light and heavy shading, pencil and both pen colours, an erased-and-changed answer, a deliberate double mark, a blank sheet, a crooked feed and a photocopy. The reader is tuned against those before it is trusted with a real cohort’s marks.

3. User Roles & Access Control

Each of the four roles signs in to its own workspace. The menu shows only what that role is meant to use, and the same rules are enforced on the server — so a typed URL, a stale bookmark or a shared link cannot reach a screen the menu withholds.

Role Signs in to Can access Cannot access
Admin
Owner / Director		 Institute Dashboard Everything — institute dashboard with the sales strip and counsellor leaderboard, all sales screens, fees and concessions, reports, and every academic module. Nothing restricted
Office Staff
Front desk, back office, accounts		 Institute Dashboard All day-to-day operations — enquiries, follow-ups, demos, admissions, fee collection, receipts, sales reports, attendance, exams, materials, OMR and tickets. Takes exam fees at the counter, including a school paying for a batch, and prints receipts. Granting discounts & scholarships; deactivating a student; the counsellor's own sales dashboard. Setting an exam's fee, waiving one, or giving a candidate their own amount — taking a payment is front-desk work, pricing it is not. Institute dashboard, management reports, attendance, exams, materials, OMR, tickets, the whole finance module (fund, vendors, work orders, expenses, exam fees), and any other counsellor's leads or figures.

Faculty		 Attendance Attendance marking & reports, student records, exams & marks, learning materials, OMR scanning, support tickets. All fee and payment screens, the entire finance module, the sales pipeline, admissions, management reports and the institute dashboard.

🔒 How the restrictions are applied

Two layers, so hiding a menu item is never the only thing stopping access.

What the user sees

  • One shared menu that renders per role — no screen is offered that the role cannot open
  • Each role lands on its own home page at sign-in; the logo returns there
  • Mobile bottom tabs follow the same rules as the desktop menu
  • Sensitive panels are withheld within a shared page — a teacher opening a student record sees the academic details but not the payment history, fee balance or source enquiry

What the server enforces

  • Every request is checked against the signed-in role before the page is built (AuthFilter)
  • A restricted address redirects the user to their own home page rather than failing
  • Counsellors are scoped at the database level — lead lists, search, demos and the reminder queue return only their own records
  • Editing another counsellor's lead, or opening it by changing the web address, is refused outright
  • Discounts, scholarships and student deactivation are restricted to Admin on submit, not merely hidden
  • The same applies inside a shared screen: office staff can take an exam fee but the actions that decide what is owed — setting the exam fee, waiving one, setting a candidate's own amount — are refused on submit, not just left off the page
  • The finance reports carry the same lock as the finance screens, so a report cannot be used to read figures the screen withholds — the export route is locked too

Finance Module — added August 2026

  • Exam fee collection by roll number: type the roll from the hall ticket, see the candidate and their balance, take the payment, print the receipt (/exam-fees, EXM- receipt series)
  • Fully-paid tracking per candidate: Paid / Part paid / Unpaid / Waived / No fee set, worked out from the fee and the receipts rather than stored, so it always matches the money actually taken
  • One school payment split across many candidates in a single transaction — allocated in roll order, one school receipt plus a receipt per child (school_receipts, SCH- series)
  • Per-candidate waivers and own amounts, with the reason recorded; neither can be applied over money already taken
  • Expense fund: credited by top-up, debited by every expense, with a running statement and reversals kept visible (/fund)
  • Vendor register: contact, GSTIN/PAN, bank details, and each vendor's ordered / paid / outstanding position (/vendors)
  • Work orders per vendor with an approval step — nothing can be paid against a draft — plus a work-order PDF to send the vendor (/work-orders, WO- series)
  • Expenses record which vendor and which work order they settle. An expense cannot exceed what the order has left, and cannot be booked against another vendor's order (/expenses, EXP- voucher series)
  • Every expense debits the fund in the same transaction; cancelling a voucher credits it back and both entries stay on the statement
  • 4 finance reports with Excel export: Exam Fee Collection, Expense Register, Vendor Outstanding, Fund Statement
  • Finance strip on the admin dashboard: fund balance, spent this month, owed to vendors, exam fees collected & due

Future Implementation

  • Refunds & cheque-bounce handling
  • GST-compliant invoices & financial-year closing
  • Bank reconciliation & settlement report for Razorpay
  • Multi-level concession approval workflow (currently admin-only)
  • Bill / invoice scan upload against each expense voucher
